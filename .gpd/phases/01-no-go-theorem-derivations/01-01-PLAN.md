  <name>Task 1: Derive the thermodynamic no-go argument</name>
  <files>derivations/nogo-thermodynamic.md</files>
  <action>
  Derive the thermodynamic no-go argument for cyclic ZPE extraction:

  1. **Setup:** Define the Casimir system — two parallel conducting plates at separation a, quantum EM field between them, Helmholtz free energy F(a, T).

  2. **Cycle definition:** A quasi-static cycle closes plates from a_max to a_min (extracting work W_close = ∫_{a_max}^{a_min} F_Casimir(a) da where F_Casimir = -∂F/∂a) then opens plates back (requiring work W_open = ∫_{a_min}^{a_max} F_Casimir(a) da).

  3. **Conservative force argument:** Since F_Casimir(a) depends only on the instantaneous separation a (and fixed material properties and temperature), it is a conservative force. Therefore W_net = W_close + W_open = ∮ F_Casimir · da = 0.

  4. **Second law generalization:** For ANY cyclic process (not just quasi-static), the Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law gives: no process whose sole result is the extraction of heat from a single reservoir and its complete conversion to work. At T=0 the vacuum is the sole reservoir → W_net ≤ 0.

  5. **Number all assumptions explicitly:**
     - A1: The vacuum + plates form a closed thermodynamic system
     - A2: The Casimir force depends only on plate separation (conservative force)
     - A3: Material properties are fixed throughout the cycle
     - A4: The second law of thermodynamics applies at T=0
     - A5: The process is cyclic (system returns to initial state)

  6. **Identify loopholes:** The argument fails if A2 is violated (material properties change mid-cycle, as in Pinto's proposal) or if A1 is violated (external energy input not accounted).
  </action>

  - Dimensional check: F_Casimir has dimensions [force/area] = [energy/length × area]; W_net has dimensions [energy/area]
  - Limiting case: For ideal plates at T=0, F = -π²/(240a⁴), and ∮ F da = 0 trivially for any closed path in a-space
  - Consistency: W_net = 0 (not just ≤ 0) for the conservative case; W_net ≤ 0 for the general case
  - Cross-check: Scandurra (2001) proved the Casimir force is conservative for fixed materials — cite
  </verify>

  <name>Task 2: Derive the Lorentz invariance no-go argument</name>
  <files>derivations/nogo-lorentz.md</files>
  <action>
  Derive the Lorentz invariance no-go argument:

  1. **Setup:** The quantum vacuum |0⟩ in free-space QFT is defined as the unique Poincaré-invariant state: U(Λ,a)|0⟩ = |0⟩ for all Lorentz transformations Λ and translations a.

  2. **Vacuum stress-energy tensor:** Since |0⟩ is Poincaré-invariant, the VEV ⟨0|T^{μν}(x)|0⟩ must be a Lorentz-invariant, translation-invariant symmetric rank-2 tensor. The ONLY such tensor is proportional to g^{μν}:
     ⟨0|T^{μν}|0⟩ = ρ_vac g^{μν}
     where ρ_vac is the vacuum energy density.

  3. **Equation of state:** Reading off components in the rest frame:
     - T^{00} = ρ_vac (energy density)
     - T^{ii} = -ρ_vac for i=1,2,3 (pressure = -ρ_vac in mostly-minus convention)
     So p_vac = -ρ_vac (dark energy equation of state).

  4. **Non-extractability:** A uniform medium with p = -ρ has the property that any expansion or compression does zero net work: dW = p dV but the energy change is dE = ρ dV, and dE + dW = (ρ - ρ)dV = 0. The vacuum "replenishes" itself — you cannot deplete it.

  5. **Number all assumptions:**
     - B1: The vacuum state is Poincaré-invariant
     - B2: No boundaries or external fields are present (free vacuum)
     - B3: ⟨0|T^{μν}|0⟩ is well-defined (regularization gives a finite, covariant result)
     - B4: The system is described by a local Poincaré-covariant QFT

  6. **Critical caveat (boundary failure):** Boundaries BREAK translational invariance. In a Casimir cavity, ⟨0|T^{μν}|0⟩ depends on position relative to the plates. Assumption B2 fails → the argument does not apply to bounded systems. This is precisely WHY the Casimir effect exists and produces a non-trivial force.

  7. **Explicitly map to loophole:** The boundary condition loophole (Phase 05, R3.1) targets assumption B2 directly.
  </action>

  - Symmetry check: g^{μν} is indeed the unique Lorentz-invariant symmetric rank-2 tensor (verify: ε^{μνρσ} is rank-4; δ^μ_ν = g^{μν} for the metric)
  - Consistency with known result: in cosmology, ρ_vac g^{μν} gives the cosmological constant term — standard result
  - Self-consistency: argument correctly identifies its own failure mode (boundaries)
  </verify>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: Derive the passivity no-go argument (Pusz-Woronowicz)</name>
  <files>derivations/nogo-passivity.md</files>
  <action>
  Derive the passivity no-go argument:

  1. **Definition of passivity:** A state ρ of a quantum system with Hamiltonian H is PASSIVE if for every cyclic unitary U (meaning the time evolution returns to the same Hamiltonian):
     Tr(ρ H) ≤ Tr(U ρ U† H)
     Equivalently: ΔE = Tr(U ρ U† H) - Tr(ρ H) ≥ 0. No cyclic operation can lower the system's energy → no work extraction.

  2. **Pusz-Woronowicz Theorem (1978):** A state ρ is passive if and only if:
     (a) ρ = f(H) for some decreasing function f (i.e., ρ commutes with H and higher energies have lower populations), OR equivalently
     (b) ρ is a Gibbs state e^{-βH}/Z for some β ≥ 0, or the ground state (β → ∞)
     Reference: Pusz & Woronowicz, Commun. Math. Phys. 58, 273 (1978)

  3. **Application to vacuum:** The QFT vacuum |0⟩ is the ground state of the field Hamiltonian H. By the Pusz-Woronowicz theorem, |0⟩ is passive. Therefore:
     ⟨0|H|0⟩ ≤ ⟨0|U†HU|0⟩ for all unitaries U
     No cyclic unitary can extract energy from the vacuum.

  4. **Proof sketch for ground state passivity (elementary):**
     Let H|n⟩ = E_n|n⟩ with E_0 ≤ E_1 ≤ E_2 ≤ ...
     For state ρ = |0⟩⟨0|:
     Tr(UρU† H) = ⟨0|U†HU|0⟩ = Σ_n |⟨n|U|0⟩|² E_n
     Since Σ_n |⟨n|U|0⟩|² = 1 (unitarity) and E_n ≥ E_0:
     Σ_n |⟨n|U|0⟩|² E_n ≥ E_0 · Σ_n |⟨n|U|0⟩|² = E_0 = ⟨0|H|0⟩
     Therefore ΔE ≥ 0. QED.

  5. **Number all assumptions:**
     - C1: The Hamiltonian H is TIME-INDEPENDENT throughout the cyclic operation
     - C2: The operation is cyclic: initial and final Hamiltonians are identical
     - C3: The vacuum |0⟩ is the unique ground state of H (non-degenerate)
     - C4: The system is quantum mechanical with well-defined H and Hilbert space
     - C5: The unitary U acts on the same Hilbert space as H

  6. **Critical gap — time-dependent Hamiltonian:** When Casimir plates move, the Hamiltonian changes: H(t) = H(a(t)). The Pusz-Woronowicz theorem requires FIXED H (assumption C1). For time-dependent H:
     - The instantaneous ground state |0(t)⟩ of H(t) tracks the Hamiltonian
     - But the actual state |ψ(t)⟩ evolves by Schrödinger equation and may NOT be the instantaneous ground state
     - Excitations created by non-adiabatic changes → energy deposited into the field
     - This is the DYNAMIC CASIMIR EFFECT — but the energy comes from the mechanical work driving the plates, not from the vacuum
     - Open question: can the gap between |ψ(t)⟩ and |0(t)⟩ ever be exploited for net extraction?

  7. **Complete passivity (stronger result):** N copies of a passive state ρ^⊗N are also passive if and only if ρ is a Gibbs state or ground state. The vacuum satisfies this (it IS the ground state). This rules out multi-copy extraction schemes.

  8. **Connection to Allahverdyan et al. (2004):** Work extraction from quantum systems is bounded by the difference between the state's energy and the passive state energy. For the vacuum (already passive), extractable work = 0.
  </action>

  - Cross-check with Lenard (1978): Independent derivation of passivity — should agree
