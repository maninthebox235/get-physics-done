# No-Go Argument C: Passivity of the Vacuum State

## Assumptions

The following assumptions are required for this argument. Each is numbered for explicit reference and loophole identification.

- **C1 (Time-independent Hamiltonian):** The Hamiltonian $H$ of the quantum field system is time-independent throughout the cyclic operation. The system's dynamics are generated by a fixed $H$ at all times.

- **C2 (Cyclic process):** The operation is cyclic: the initial and final Hamiltonians are identical. Formally, the time evolution is generated by a unitary operator $U$ such that $H(t_f) = H(t_i) = H$.

- **C3 (Non-degenerate ground state):** The vacuum $|0\rangle$ is the unique ground state of $H$: $H|0\rangle = E_0|0\rangle$ with $E_0 < E_1 \leq E_2 \leq \cdots$, where $\{E_n\}$ are the eigenvalues of $H$. There is no degeneracy at the ground state level.

- **C4 (Quantum mechanical framework):** The system is described by quantum mechanics with a well-defined Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ and a self-adjoint Hamiltonian $H$ bounded from below. The time evolution is unitary.

- **C5 (Same Hilbert space):** The cyclic unitary $U$ acts on the same Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ as the Hamiltonian $H$. No degrees of freedom are added or removed during the process.

## Definition of Passivity

A state $\rho$ of a quantum system with Hamiltonian $H$ is called **passive** (Pusz & Woronowicz, 1978) if no cyclic unitary operation can lower its expected energy:

$$
\text{Tr}(\rho\, H) \leq \text{Tr}(U\rho\, U^\dagger H) \quad \forall\ U \in \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
\tag{C.1}
$$

Equivalently, the energy change under any cyclic unitary satisfies:

$$
\Delta E \equiv \text{Tr}(U\rho\, U^\dagger H) - \text{Tr}(\rho\, H) \geq 0
\tag{C.2}
$$

**Physical meaning:** If $\Delta E \geq 0$ for all unitaries $U$, then no cyclic process can extract energy from the state $\rho$. The maximum extractable work from a state $\rho$ under cyclic unitaries is called the **ergotropy** (Allahverdyan et al., 2004):

$$
\mathcal{W}(\rho, H) = \text{Tr}(\rho\, H) - \min_{U}\, \text{Tr}(U\rho\, U^\dagger H)
\tag{C.3}
$$

A state is passive if and only if $\mathcal{W}(\rho, H) = 0$.

**Dimensional check:** $[\text{Tr}(\rho\, H)] = [\text{energy}]$. The ergotropy $\mathcal{W}$ has dimensions of energy. Correct.

## The Pusz-Woronowicz Theorem (1978)

**Theorem** (Pusz & Woronowicz, Commun. Math. Phys. 58, 273 (1978); see also Lenard, J. Stat. Phys. 19, 575 (1978)):

A state $\rho$ is passive with respect to a Hamiltonian $H$ if and only if:

(a) $\rho$ commutes with $H$: $[\rho, H] = 0$, and

(b) $\rho$ is a decreasing function of $H$: if $E_n < E_m$, then $p_n \geq p_m$, where $\rho = \sum_n p_n |n\rangle\langle n|$ in the energy eigenbasis.

Equivalently, $\rho$ is passive if and only if it is a Gibbs state $\rho = e^{-\beta H}/Z$ for some $\beta \geq 0$ (with $\beta = 0$ being the completely mixed state and $\beta \to \infty$ being the ground state), or a convex combination of Gibbs states with a decreasing population distribution.

**Reference:** Pusz & Woronowicz (1978) (ref-pusz-woronowicz) established this characterization. Lenard (1978) (ref-lenard) independently proved equivalent results.

## Application to the Vacuum State

The QFT vacuum $|0\rangle$ is the ground state of the field Hamiltonian $H$. As a pure state, $\rho_{\text{vac}} = |0\rangle\langle 0|$. This trivially satisfies conditions (a) and (b) of the Pusz-Woronowicz theorem:

(a) $[\rho_{\text{vac}}, H] = [|0\rangle\langle 0|, H] = E_0|0\rangle\langle 0| - E_0|0\rangle\langle 0| = 0$. Commutes.

(b) The population is $p_0 = 1$, $p_n = 0$ for $n \geq 1$. Since $E_0 \leq E_n$ and $p_0 \geq p_n$, the population is a (weakly) decreasing function of energy. Satisfies the condition.

Therefore, the vacuum $|0\rangle$ is a passive state. By Eq. (C.2):

$$
\boxed{\Delta E = \text{Tr}(U\rho_{\text{vac}} U^\dagger H) - \text{Tr}(\rho_{\text{vac}} H) \geq 0 \quad \forall\ U}
\tag{C.4}
$$

No cyclic unitary operation can extract energy from the vacuum.

**SELF-CRITIQUE CHECKPOINT (step 1):**
1. SIGN CHECK: $\Delta E \geq 0$ means energy increases or stays the same. Consistent with no extraction ($W_{\text{net}} \leq 0$).
2. FACTOR CHECK: No numerical factors introduced. Pure algebraic argument.
3. CONVENTION CHECK: $W_{\text{net}} > 0$ = extraction convention. Since extracted work $= -\Delta E \leq 0$, we have $W_{\text{net}} \leq 0$. Consistent.
4. DIMENSION CHECK: $[\Delta E] = [\text{energy}]$. Correct.

## Elementary Proof of Ground State Passivity

We provide a self-contained proof that the ground state is passive, independent of the full Pusz-Woronowicz machinery.

**Theorem:** Let $H$ have eigenvalues $E_0 \leq E_1 \leq E_2 \leq \cdots$ with eigenstates $|n\rangle$. The ground state $|0\rangle$ is passive: $\langle 0|U^\dagger H U|0\rangle \geq E_0$ for all unitaries $U$.

**Proof:**

Let $U$ be any unitary on $\mathcal{H}$. Define $|\psi\rangle = U|0\rangle$, which is a normalized state since $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = \langle 0|U^\dagger U|0\rangle = 1$.

Expand $|\psi\rangle$ in the energy eigenbasis:

$$
|\psi\rangle = U|0\rangle = \sum_n c_n |n\rangle, \quad c_n = \langle n|U|0\rangle
\tag{C.5}
$$

By unitarity:

$$
\sum_n |c_n|^2 = \langle 0|U^\dagger U|0\rangle = 1
\tag{C.6}
$$

The expected energy in the transformed state is:

$$
\langle 0|U^\dagger H U|0\rangle = \langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle = \sum_n |c_n|^2 E_n
\tag{C.7}
$$

Since $E_n \geq E_0$ for all $n$ (by definition of the ground state):

$$
\sum_n |c_n|^2 E_n \geq E_0 \sum_n |c_n|^2 = E_0
\tag{C.8}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\langle 0|U^\dagger H U|0\rangle \geq E_0 = \langle 0|H|0\rangle
\tag{C.9}
$$

which gives $\Delta E \geq 0$. QED.

**Equality holds** if and only if $U|0\rangle = e^{i\alpha}|0\rangle$ for some phase $\alpha$ (i.e., $U$ maps the ground state to itself up to a phase), assuming the ground state is non-degenerate (C3).

**SELF-CRITIQUE CHECKPOINT (step 2):**
1. SIGN CHECK: Inequality direction $\geq$ correct throughout. $E_n \geq E_0$ used correctly.
2. FACTOR CHECK: No factors introduced.
3. CONVENTION CHECK: Still using $W_{\text{net}} > 0$ = extraction. The result $\Delta E \geq 0$ means work input required, so $W_{\text{net}} \leq 0$. Consistent.
4. DIMENSION CHECK: All terms in Eq. (C.7)-(C.9) have dimension $[\text{energy}]$. Correct.

### Verification: Two-Level System

As a limiting case, consider a two-level system with $H = E_0|0\rangle\langle 0| + E_1|1\rangle\langle 1|$, $E_0 < E_1$.

The most general unitary on a 2D Hilbert space is:

$$
U = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\alpha}\cos\theta & -e^{i\beta}\sin\theta \\ e^{-i\beta}\sin\theta & e^{-i\alpha}\cos\theta \end{pmatrix}
\tag{C.10}
$$

Then $U|0\rangle = e^{i\alpha}\cos\theta\,|0\rangle + e^{-i\beta}\sin\theta\,|1\rangle$, and:

$$
\langle 0|U^\dagger H U|0\rangle = \cos^2\theta\, E_0 + \sin^2\theta\, E_1 = E_0 + \sin^2\theta\,(E_1 - E_0)
\tag{C.11}
$$

Since $E_1 - E_0 > 0$ and $\sin^2\theta \geq 0$:

$$
\langle 0|U^\dagger H U|0\rangle \geq E_0
\tag{C.12}
$$

with equality only at $\theta = 0$ (trivial unitary, up to phases). Any non-trivial mixing increases the energy. The ground state is passive. Confirmed.

## Complete Passivity

A stronger result holds: the ground state is **completely passive**.

**Definition:** A state $\rho$ is completely passive if $\rho^{\otimes N}$ is passive for all $N \geq 1$. That is, even with $N$ identical copies of the system, no joint cyclic unitary on the combined Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}$ can extract work.

**Theorem** (Pusz & Woronowicz, 1978): A state is completely passive if and only if it is a Gibbs state $\rho = e^{-\beta H}/Z$ for some $\beta \geq 0$, or the ground state ($\beta \to \infty$).

The vacuum $|0\rangle$ is the ground state, hence it is completely passive. This rules out multi-copy extraction schemes: even with access to $N$ identical Casimir cavities, no joint cyclic unitary can extract net work from the collection.

**Physical significance:** Some passive states that are not completely passive allow work extraction when multiple copies are available (Alicki & Fannes, 2013). The ground state does not have this vulnerability.

## Connection to Extractable Work

Allahverdyan, Balian, and Nieuwenhuizen (2004) showed that the maximum extractable work from a quantum state $\rho$ under cyclic unitary operations is:

$$
\mathcal{W}(\rho, H) = \text{Tr}(\rho\, H) - \text{Tr}(\sigma_\rho H)
\tag{C.13}
$$

where $\sigma_\rho$ is the passive state with the same spectrum as $\rho$ (eigenvalues rearranged in decreasing order relative to energy). For the vacuum (already passive), $\sigma_{\rho_{\text{vac}}} = \rho_{\text{vac}}$, so:

$$
\mathcal{W}(\rho_{\text{vac}}, H) = 0
\tag{C.14}
$$

The extractable work from the vacuum is exactly zero.

**SELF-CRITIQUE CHECKPOINT (step 3):**
1. SIGN CHECK: $\mathcal{W} = 0$ for the vacuum. No extraction. Consistent.
2. FACTOR CHECK: No factors.
3. CONVENTION CHECK: $W_{\text{net}} > 0$ = extraction. Extractable work $= 0$, so $W_{\text{net}} = 0 \leq 0$. Consistent.
4. DIMENSION CHECK: $[\mathcal{W}] = [\text{energy}]$. Correct.

## Critical Gap: Time-Dependent Hamiltonian

**The passivity argument assumes a FIXED Hamiltonian (C1). This assumption fails for a Casimir system with moving plates.**

When the plates move, the Hamiltonian changes with time: $H(t) = H(a(t))$, where $a(t)$ is the plate separation. The key issues are:

### 1. Instantaneous Ground State vs. Actual State

At each moment $t$, the Hamiltonian $H(a(t))$ has an instantaneous ground state $|0(t)\rangle$:

$$
H(a(t))|0(t)\rangle = E_0(a(t))|0(t)\rangle
\tag{C.15}
$$

However, the actual state $|\psi(t)\rangle$ of the system evolves by the Schrodinger equation:

$$
i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi(t)\rangle = H(a(t))|\psi(t)\rangle
\tag{C.16}
$$

In general, $|\psi(t)\rangle \neq |0(t)\rangle$. The system does not remain in the instantaneous ground state when the Hamiltonian changes.

### 2. The Adiabatic Theorem

If the plates move **sufficiently slowly** compared to the field's energy gap $\Delta E(a) = E_1(a) - E_0(a)$, the adiabatic theorem guarantees that $|\psi(t)\rangle \approx e^{i\phi(t)}|0(t)\rangle$: the system tracks the instantaneous ground state (up to a phase).

The adiabatic condition is:

$$
\left|\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right| \ll \frac{[\Delta E(a)]^2}{|\langle 1(a)|\partial_a H|0(a)\rangle|}
\tag{C.17}
$$

When the adiabatic condition is satisfied and the process is cyclic ($a(t_f) = a(t_i)$), the system returns to the original ground state. No excitations are created, and the net work is:

$$
W_{\text{net}} = \oint \frac{\partial E_0(a)}{\partial a}\, da = 0
\tag{C.18}
$$

because $E_0(a)$ is a state function. This recovers the conservative force result (Argument A).

### 3. The Dynamic Casimir Effect

If the plates move **rapidly** compared to the energy gap (non-adiabatic motion), the time-dependent Hamiltonian creates real excitations of the quantum field --- photons produced from the vacuum. This is the **dynamic Casimir effect** (Moore, 1970; Fulling & Davies, 1976).

Key points:
- The photon energy comes from the **mechanical work** done on the plates to accelerate them, NOT from the vacuum energy itself.
- The vacuum acts as a **transducer**, converting mechanical energy into photon radiation via parametric amplification of vacuum fluctuations.
- The energy budget is: $W_{\text{mech}} = E_{\text{photons}} + E_{\text{kinetic}}$. The vacuum provides zero net energy.

### 4. Open Question

**Can the gap between $|\psi(t)\rangle$ and $|0(t)\rangle$ ever be exploited for net energy extraction?**

The passivity theorem guarantees no extraction for fixed $H$, and the adiabatic theorem guarantees no extraction for slowly varying $H$. For rapidly varying $H$:

- The dynamic Casimir effect produces photons, but the energy comes from the driving mechanism.
- However, the precise energy accounting for a complete cycle with non-adiabatic segments has subtleties, particularly regarding whether the photon radiation alters the effective force on the plates during the return stroke.
- This open question connects to Phase 06 (dynamic Casimir loophole analysis).

## Mapping to the No-Go Structure

$$
\boxed{W_{\text{net}} \leq 0 \quad \text{(passivity of the ground state, fixed } H\text{)}}
\tag{C.19}
$$

The passivity argument gives the strongest no-go for systems with time-independent Hamiltonians, but its applicability to the physically relevant Casimir scenario (moving plates, time-dependent $H$) requires the additional assumption of adiabaticity. The non-adiabatic regime (dynamic Casimir effect) requires separate analysis (Phase 06).
